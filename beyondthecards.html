Beyond the Cards

Turning a fragmented workflow into one connected workspace

A shift from opening individual cards and navigating back between records to selecting a row and viewing its details in one place. A focused interaction change that made a complex, high-volume workflow simpler and more consistent across the product.

The project

One workflow. Too much back and forth.

The existing ETL Jobs experience presented records as cards. Each card exposed a small amount of information and required users to open a separate view to work with it. Moving to another record meant returning to the list and opening the next card.

My contribution

UX direction, interaction redesign, AI-assisted exploration and close design-engineering collaboration.

Delivery window

Three weeks of continuous collaboration and daily connects with engineering.

Focus

ETL Jobs workflow and the way users move between a record list and its configuration details.

Approach

Reuse an established table pattern already familiar across other product modules.

The challenge

The friction was in the interaction model

The cards made each job feel like a separate destination. That pattern created repeated navigation for a task that often involved moving across multiple records and reviewing their configuration.

What users had to do

  1. Find the required job card.
  2. Open it to access the configuration.
  3. Review or update the details.
  4. Go back to the card listing.
  5. Open another card to continue.

Why it mattered

  • Repeated open-and-return navigation interrupted the task.
  • Users had to reorient themselves each time they moved to another record.
  • The card layout differed from table-based patterns used elsewhere in the product.
  • The experience made a record-management task feel more fragmented than it needed to be.
The key realization
Users did not need a separate destination for every record. They needed a clear way to move through the list while keeping the selected record's details accessible.

The design decision

From cards to a master-detail table

We replaced the card-based navigation with a table-led layout. The left side became the working list of ETL jobs. Selecting a row brought that job's details into the panel on the right.

Click a row→ Review or edit

What changed in the experience

  • One-click access: Selecting a row displays the corresponding details on the right.
  • Less navigation: Users can move from one record to another without repeatedly leaving and returning to the list.
  • Clearer information structure: The table supports scanning and locating records in a consistent format.
  • Familiar product behavior: The interaction aligns with table-based patterns already used in other modules.
The important part was not the table itself.
It was keeping the list and the selected record's details available together, so users could move through their work without losing context.

How we made it happen

Three weeks. Daily connects. One shared direction.

This was not a design-and-handoff exercise. We worked closely with engineering through three weeks of continuous daily connects, resolving questions and refining the experience as the solution took shape.

Align

Establish the interaction problem and agree on the table-led direction, using familiar patterns from the existing product.

Explore with AI

Leverage AI to accelerate design exploration and iteration, helping the team move through the change more quickly.

Work through delivery

Connect with engineering every day, review the evolving implementation, resolve details and keep design intent aligned with what was being built.

How AI contributed
AI helped us accelerate the change. It supported faster exploration and iteration, while the design decisions, product-context checks and trade-offs remained human-led and closely connected to engineering.

The outcome

A small interaction change with a larger operational impact

The redesign addressed a recurring source of friction in the workflow. By reducing the need to open and close separate views and by bringing the experience in line with familiar product patterns, the change contributed to lower operational effort and better adoption.

Operational effort

Less repeated navigation to access and move between job records and their details.

Product adoption

A simpler, more familiar interaction model aligned with patterns already present across modules.

Customer and business value

A meaningful improvement to a core product workflow, with significant impact for the company and its customers.

Reflection

Good design can make complexity feel lighter

The most valuable change was not adding more functionality. It was changing how existing information was organized and accessed. A familiar table pattern, a persistent details panel and a close working rhythm with engineering turned a fragmented sequence into a more connected workflow.

This project reinforced an important principle in my approach to product design: sometimes the biggest improvement comes from questioning the interaction model, not adding another layer to the interface.
